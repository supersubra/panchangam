Understanding Sankalpam பொதுத் தகவல்

A Sankalpam is a solemn statement of intention before a puja or other ritual. It identifies when, where, who and for what purpose: the time, the place, the person and the worship being undertaken.

Its wording moves from vast cycles of sacred time and geography to our own day and home. This guide uses India, Tamil Nadu and Chennai as its example.

Where am I? The place terms

The familiar Indian wording begins with Jambu Dweepe, Bharata Varshe, Bharata Khande. These are traditional sacred-geography terms; they do not form a precise map of today's political boundaries.

The traditional counts: 7 great Dweepas in the Puranic world-picture; 9 Varshas within Jambudvipa; and 9 divisions, often called Khandas, within Bharata Varsha. The two lists of nine describe different levels, not nine regions for the whole Earth.

Dvipa / Dweepam — the great landmass
An island or landmass surrounded by water. The seven great Dweepas (Sapta Dvipa) are Jambu, Plaksha, Shalmali, Kusha, Krauncha, Shaka and Pushkara. Jambudvipa contains Bharata Varsha; Jambu Dweepe means “in Jambudvipa”.
All 7 Dweepas and modern continent comparisons

The Puranic account describes landmasses surrounded by successive seas. There is no established one-to-one identification with today's seven continents. The modern names below show the convention used by this app's Sankalpa wording, not verified ancient geographical equivalents.

DweepaModern continent used by this app (convention only)
1. JambuAsia, including India
2. PlakshaEurope
3. ShalmaliSouth America
4. KushaAfrica
5. KraunchaNorth America
6. ShakaAustralia / Oceania
7. PushkaraAntarctica

Other traditions use different overseas names. In particular, “Pushkara = Antarctica” is only an app convention, not a geographical identification established by the Puranas.

Traditional list: Vishnu Purana, Book II, Chapter II.

Varsha / Varshe — the broad region
A region within a dvipa. Jambudvipa has nine Varshas in this account. Bharata Varshe means “in the region of Bharata”, described as south of the Himalayas and north of the ocean. Here, varsha means a region, not a year.
All 9 Varshas of Jambudvipa and possible modern comparisons

Bharata has a clear broad association with the Indian subcontinent. The other regions belong to the sacred geography around Meru. The comparisons below are interpretive suggestions; they are not agreed modern borders.

VarshaApproximate association or what is known
1. BharataThe Indian subcontinent; broader than the modern Republic of India.
2. KimpurushaNepal / the Himalayan region is a proposed comparison.
3. HarivarshaWestern Tibet is a proposed comparison.
4. IlavritaThe central region around Meru; the Pamir region of Central Asia is one proposed comparison.
5. RamyakaNorth of Ilavrita in the traditional account; modern location uncertain.
6. HiranmayaFarther north, beyond Ramyaka; modern location uncertain.
7. UttarakuruThe far-northern region in the traditional account; no agreed modern country.
8. BhadrashvaEast of Meru; no agreed modern country.
9. KetumalaWest of Meru; no agreed modern country.

Sources: Vishnu Purana II.2 for the traditional arrangement; Vayu Purana, Chapter 46, translation notes for the proposed Nepal, western Tibet and Pamir comparisons.

This is the list within Jambudvipa, not a fixed count for every Dweepa. The app's overseas phrase “Ramanaka Varshe” is a separate ritual adaptation; it should not be read as a proven identification of Ramyaka with America.

Khanda / Khande — the division or territory
Khanda means a part or division. A common account gives nine divisions of Bharata Varsha, also called Dvipas in the texts. Bharata Khande (often written “Bharata Kande” in Tamil usage) identifies the land of Bharata in the customary Sankalpa formula.
All 9 divisions of Bharata and proposed modern locations

The list below follows the Bharata divisions in the Vishnu and Brahma Puranas. Names vary between texts. The modern comparisons for the first eight follow historical proposals attributed to Alexander Cunningham in the linked study; these remain uncertain and other scholars disagree.

Division / KhandaProposed modern association (not established)
1. IndradvipaMyanmar (formerly Burma).
2. Kaserumat / KasetumanThe Malay Peninsula.
3. Tamraparna / TamravarnaSri Lanka (formerly Ceylon).
4. GabhastimatLakshadweep, the Maldives or the Ernakulam area of Kerala have been suggested; no single settled match.
5. NagadvipaSalsette, Elephanta and Kathiawar in western India have been suggested.
6. SaumyaKutch, Gujarat.
7. GandharvaThe Gandhara / Kabul region, roughly parts of present-day Afghanistan and Pakistan.
8. VarunaNortheastern India in this proposal; other interpretations place it on India's western coast.
9. BharataThe Indian mainland. The Vishnu Purana leaves the ninth division unnamed; the Brahma Purana calls it Bharata.

Why “proposed”? For example, another interpretation connects Tamraparna with the part of southern India below the Kaveri, rather than Sri Lanka. These lists should not be used as a modern atlas.

Sources: Vishnu Purana II.3; Brahma Purana 17 and its geographical notes; Brahma Purana: A Critical Study, “The area of Bharata”, reporting Cunningham's comparisons.

For Chennai: the customary sequence remains Jambu Dweepe → Bharata Varshe → Bharata Khande → Tamil Nadu → Chennai. Tamil Nadu is the state named later in the wording, not a separate member of this nine-Khanda list.

Meroh dakshina digbhage — south of Meru
This places the worshipper south of Mount Meru in traditional cosmology. It is a sacred point of reference, not a modern street address.
Maha khanda — a great land division
Some present-day Sankalpa versions add the modern continent here. This is an additional location description, not a compulsory phrase in every Indian family tradition.

In these Sanskrit phrases, the ending “-e” expresses “in”: Dweepe, Varshe and Khande. A literal word meaning and its use in a particular ritual formula are not always identical.

From Bharata to a home in Chennai

The place can then be named more closely: Desha (country), Pradesha (state or region), Nagara (city) and Griha (home).

Illustrative place passage for a puja in one's own home; this is not the complete mantra or a live reading of your Settings.

Jambu Dweepe, Bharata Varshe, Bharata Khande, Meroh dakshina digbhage, Bharata deshe, Tamil Nadu pradeshe, Chennai nagare, mama sva-grihe.

Tamil-script reading of the example:

ஜம்பூ த்வீபே, பாரத வர்ஷே, பாரத கண்டே, மேரோ: தக்ஷிண திக்பாகே, பாரத தேஶே, தமிழ்நாடு ப்ரதேஶே, சென்னை நகரே, மம ஸ்வக்ருஹே.

Meaning: In Jambudvipa, in the region and territory of Bharata, south of Meru, in India, in Tamil Nadu, in Chennai, in my own home.

For a temple or another venue, the final place wording changes. Families may also name a nearby sacred river or kshetra. Follow the wording used in your family tradition.

When is the ritual? The cosmic time terms

The opening time phrases describe the traditional Hindu cosmic calendar. All durations below are in earthly (human) years, using the traditional Puranic reckoning.

Dvitiya parardhe
In the second half of Brahma's lifespan — 311.04 trillion earthly years (311,040,000,000,000 years). One half, called a Parardha, is 155.52 trillion earthly years (155,520,000,000,000 years).
Shveta-Varaha kalpe
In the kalpa called Shveta Varaha. One Kalpa, the daytime of Brahma, is 4.32 billion earthly years (4,320,000,000 years; 432 crore). His night lasts equally long, so one full day and night is 8.64 billion earthly years.
Vaivasvata manvantare
In the period of Vaivasvata Manu, the seventh Manu of this kalpa. A manvantara is an era governed by a Manu. One Manvantara is 306.72 million earthly years (306,720,000 years; 30.672 crore), or 71 Mahayugas, excluding the transitional intervals called Sandhyas.
Ashtavimshatitame — the twenty-eighth cycle
In the twenty-eighth Mahayuga of the present manvantara. One Mahayuga (Chaturyuga) is 4.32 million earthly years (4,320,000 years; 43.2 lakh), comprising Krita (Satya), Treta, Dvapara and Kali Yuga.
Kaliyuge, prathame pade
In Kali Yuga, in its first quarter. Kali Yuga lasts 432,000 earthly years (4.32 lakh); one quarter of that duration is 108,000 earthly years (1.08 lakh). “First quarter” names the period we are in; it does not mean that 108,000 years have already elapsed.
The four Yugas — earthly years
  • Krita / Satya Yuga: 1,728,000 years (17.28 lakh).
  • Treta Yuga: 1,296,000 years (12.96 lakh).
  • Dvapara Yuga: 864,000 years (8.64 lakh).
  • Kali Yuga: 432,000 years (4.32 lakh).
These standard totals include each yuga's opening and closing twilight periods.

How the larger periods fit: a Kalpa contains 14 Manvantaras and 15 transitional Sandhyas of 1,728,000 earthly years each, totalling 4.32 billion years. One Brahma year comprises 360 of his full days and nights: 3.1104 trillion earthly years. His lifespan is 100 such years.

Source and conversion rules: Vishnu Purana, Book I, Chapter III: measures of time. One lakh = 100,000; one crore = 10,000,000.

From cosmic time to today's Panchangam

Samvatsara, Ayana and Ritu
The named year in the 60-year cycle; Uttarayana or Dakshinayana, the half-year; and one of the six traditional seasons.
Masa and Paksha
The month and the Moon's waxing fortnight (Shukla) or waning fortnight (Krishna). This app lets you choose a lunar month or Tamil solar month in Settings.
Tithi and Vasara
The lunar day, determined by the angle between the Moon and Sun, and the weekday.
Nakshatra, Yoga and Karana
The Moon's stellar division, the Sun–Moon longitude combination, and a half-tithi. Together with Tithi and Vasara, these are the five limbs of the Panchangam.

The person and the purpose

The Sankalpa then names the worshipper and gotra, states the intention and deity, and declares the ritual to be performed. The purpose is to begin consciously, with a clear resolve.

Wording varies by family and ritual tradition. Overseas adaptations of dvipa, varsha and khanda also differ; there is no single universally agreed mapping to modern continents.
